Successcurve LMS · Redesign plan

Rebuilding the student side of an exam-prep LMS, page by page.

Scope agreed: the full student journey end to end, plus the admin and faculty dashboards. Desktop first, English only, trust-led homepage. This plan states what exists in the repository today, what changes, and in what order.

1 · What the codebase contains today

A Laravel application, Blade templates, Bootstrap 4 markup, jQuery, Owl Carousel, Plyr for video, CKEditor for authoring. 223 view files across four role areas and three shared layouts (homelayout, layout, layout3), plus a separate exam layout.

The data model is already rich enough to support real analytics: Result, Exam_answer, Subjectanalysi, Questionreport, Questiontag, course/test/series enrolments, purchases and coupons, week/lecture structure, and a doubts thread model. Very little of that reaches the student as insight today.

2 · What is actually wrong

The homepage sells nothing and teaches nothing

An admin-uploaded carousel opens the page, the search box sits below the fold, the three counters are hardcoded in the template, and the same card component repeats four times with no hierarchy between courses, tests and series.

The student dashboard is a list, not a dashboard

It answers "what did I buy", not "how am I doing" or "what do I do next". Subject analysis and per-question timing exist in the database and never surface.

No single content spine

Class, subject, course, test and series are five parallel entry points with five different card treatments. A student cannot see "my class → my subject → everything in it".

Three layouts, four sidebars, no shared vocabulary

Two 70 KB stylesheets (style.main.css, style.main02.css) plus a third override file. Buttons, badges and cards differ per page, so a fix in one place never propagates.

Nothing brings a student back tomorrow

No streak, no goal, no next-step prompt, no reminder. Every visit starts from a cold homepage.

3 · The design system to build first

Before any page is redrawn, one small system, applied everywhere:

  • Type — one display face for headings, one text face for UI; five sizes, two weights. Nothing below 13 px, body copy at 16 px.
  • Colour — one brand colour, one accent, a five-step neutral ramp, and four fixed semantic colours for correct, wrong, skipped and marked-for-review, used identically in the exam engine, the answer key and every chart.
  • Spacing and radius — a 4 px scale and two radii, so density is deliberate rather than per-page.
  • Twelve components — content card (one component for course, test and series, three variants), class chip, subject tile, price badge, progress ring, stat block, section header, table, filter bar, empty state, sidebar, question palette.

5 · Student dashboard

Three bands, in this order, because the question a returning student asks is always "what now", then "how am I doing", then "what is coming".

Band one — today

Resume card for the exact lecture or test in progress. Daily goal with a progress ring, current streak, and time studied against target. Two or three weak topics detected from subject analysis, each with a one-tap action: revise the lecture, or attempt ten questions on it.

Band two — performance

Readiness meter for the target exam. Accuracy and average speed per subject and topic. Attempted, correct, wrong and skipped as one stacked bar per test. Rank and percentile trend across the series. Your accuracy against the topper and the cohort average on the same axis.

Band three — ahead

Learning path for the class, showing chapters cleared, current and locked. Upcoming tests and deadlines with countdowns. Course and module completion per enrolment. Badges earned and the next one in reach. Class leaderboard, opt-in, never punitive.

Every number here maps to a table that already exists. Two need new writes: a daily activity log for streaks and study time, and a reminders table.

6 · Admin dashboard

One screen with a date range and a class filter, in three columns of panels.

Revenue

Courses, tests and series split, against the previous period, with refunds visible.

Enrolment funnel

Visit, signup, free attempt, purchase — conversion at each step, by class.

Active learners

Daily and weekly actives, week-on-week retention cohorts.

Completion and drop-off

Per course, the lecture where students stop. Names the fix, not just the rate.

Test analytics

Attempts, pass rate, mean score, abandonment mid-test.

Question-bank health

Reported questions, difficulty drift, near-duplicates, untagged items.

Content-team output

Questions authored and reviewed per person, lectures published, review backlog.

At-risk watchlist

Paid students inactive 7+ days or falling in accuracy, with a contact action.

Doubts queue

Open doubts, oldest unanswered, median response time by subject.

The 91 admin CRUD pages are not redrawn individually. They inherit one table pattern, one form pattern and one filter bar, which is what makes them consistent.

7 · Faculty dashboard

Narrower on purpose: my courses and their completion, my tests and how students scored, which of my questions were reported, the doubts waiting on me with their age, and one shortcut row for adding a lecture, a test or questions. The faculty and admin course tooling is duplicated in the repo; the redesign gives them one shared set of screens with permissions deciding what shows.

8 · Engagement, kept honest

Streaks and daily goals, badges for milestones that took effort, an opt-in class leaderboard, a learning-path roadmap per class, progress rings on every enrolment, and study reminders tied to real exam dates. Each one has to change behaviour, not just decorate: a badge is awarded for clearing a weak topic, not for logging in.
